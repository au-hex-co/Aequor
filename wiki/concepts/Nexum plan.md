# Nexum plan

A working record of the full reasoning trail behind [[Nexum]], the Malum, and the death of [[Nexus]] — everything discussed across this session, in order. The finished canon lives on the actual pages ([[Nexum]], [[Nexus]], [[Tellus]], [[Silvia]], [[Corvinus]], [[Cosmology]]); this page is *why* it ended up that way, kept for continuity rather than as the source of truth itself. If this page and a character/concept page ever disagree, the character/concept page wins.

## 1. The problem this was built to solve

An outside reader (the user's orthodox-Christian friend) critiqued Aequor's existing lore as reading feminist to the point men aren't structurally needed: reproduction and protection both run through [[Vesta]]'s matrilineal line and the [[Sacrarium]], and men can't hold it at all — sons only carry an inert, purely-reactive "residue" of it. The goal was a second magic system that gives men a cosmological anchor of **equal structural weight**, not a second copy of the same mechanic reskinned.

## 2. First draft: the origin sequence

The initial plan (worked out and confirmed via clarifying questions in an earlier part of this session):

1. Tellus once vows to protect the five-year-old Nexus.
2. They argue; Tellus tries to restrain him.
3. This triggers Nexus's dormant Sacrarium residue (self-defense) *and* breaks Tellus's oath — the two compound and rip Tellus apart.
4. Tellus's survival instinct fuses his essence into Nexus rather than let him die — a ditch effort, not a discovered power.
5. This fusion makes Nexus ageless: the first Fae King.
6. Centuries pass.
7. Nexus is eventually killed by "clever wordplay/trickery, turning a god's own magic against itself" — mechanism deliberately left open.
8. Nexus's death releases Tellus, weakened and half mad.
9. What sheds from Nexus at that moment is Nexum itself.
10. Tellus rampages (the Aetas of Manus) until [[Aequor]] kills him for good — the Malum, Year 0.
11. This reframes [[Anima]]'s earlier fate: maimed and sealed, not killed, distinguishing him from Tellus's final death.

## 3. The council pressure-test

This draft was run through a five-advisor LLM council (Contrarian, First Principles, Expansionist, Outsider, Executor) plus a peer-review round, at the user's request ("council this plan... find logical inconsistencies"). Findings, condensed:

- **The one genuine logic error** (four of five peer reviewers converged on this independently): step 3's oath-backlash can't run on Nexum's rules, since the plan itself states Nexum doesn't exist as a law until centuries later, at Nexus's death. Backlash from a law that doesn't exist yet is circular.
- **Soft spots, not contradictions:** the double-coincidence of two magic systems triggering on one act; the fusion reading as an unearned "authorial rescue"; the centuries-long gap between fusion and Nexus's death left totally blank; Aequor's motive for killing rather than restraining Tellus never examined; whether Nexus getting no real agency in his own origin story actually answers the original "equal weight" complaint, or just restages the same "acted upon, not acting" problem at cosmic scale.
- **What the council said worked:** the core image (a father-figure's own broken promise nearly kills him, a child's terror as unwitting co-conspirator); the Anima/Tellus maimed-vs-killed parallel; Aequor as the one who ends both brothers as a genuine character-defining pattern rather than coincidence.

Full council verdict is not reproduced here — this section is the summary that mattered for what changed next.

## 4. Resolving the council's objections

The user resolved every substantive point directly, each one tightening the logic rather than patching around it:

1. **The backlash is Tellus's own nature, not Nexum.** Tellus doesn't merely hold the domain of duty/oath — he *is* what it is, the source fate drew that gift from before it was given to [[Primum]]. Nothing outranks him, so nothing restrains him from breaking his own word — but nothing can violate what it *is* and remain whole either. This is why breaking an oath fractures **him** specifically and uniquely: ordinary people, once Nexum exists, are *weaker* than the law and can be physically overpowered by it; Tellus predates the law and is what it was drawn from, so the cost of his violation is internal (identity unraveling) rather than external restraint. This single move resolves the retroactive-causality problem entirely — nothing about Nexum needs to exist yet.
2. **The two forces aren't coincidental — they're the same act from two directions.** Restraining Nexus is an external assault (the Sacrarium residue lashing out) *and* an internal collapse (Tellus's own oath-break) at once. Neither alone would have ended him; it's the combination — an assault he can't out-endure, layered on a collapse he can't stop — that does.
3. **The fusion is explicitly a one-off, not a mechanic.** User confirmed: "I can't establish this in the story because it's a unique circumstance." No other character gets to use it; Tellus's own final death, with no second refuge available, is the proof it doesn't repeat.
4. **Nexum's active/formal tier was specified**, resolving a standing open thread: a passive contract can be broken, at a cost; a formal, deliberately invoked oath cannot — Nexum physically restrains the body of anyone who tries to violate it, and an oath can be sworn to *grant* its own terms as well as forbid their violation. This is also what explains point 1: ordinary people are weaker than Nexum and can be overpowered by it; Tellus wasn't, because he predates it.
5. **Nexus is not the hero of his own origin story, on purpose.** "He isn't the hero, he's a poor soul corrupted by the mad dragon Tellus... a means to an end." The "equal weight to Vesta" goal is met at the *system* level (Nexum as duty's domain answering Sacrarium's agency-domain, per the founding creation myth's existing man/duty, woman/agency split) rather than by making Nexus personally heroic in this specific scene.
6. **Aequor's final kill is forced, not chosen.** Binding a dragon takes two; by the time Tellus rampages, [[Anima]] is already sealed and Tellus himself is the target, leaving Aequor the only unsealed, uncorrupted dragon left in the world. No second binder existed anywhere — not a preference for violence, the only actor left standing, both times.
7. **The asymmetry between Anima's dignified, chosen sacrifice and Tellus's panicked, failed one is deliberate.** User's own reasoning: repeating the same noble-sacrifice shape for every god's ending would read as a formula the story reaches for on demand ("if each time I needed something I sacrificed a dragon, that would be convenient and bad writing"), not two deaths earning two different weights.

## 5. Ichor, blood, and the color key

Prompted by a re-read of [[Lore in order]] Ch. 3 (Aequor to Silvia: *"Let go of your Sacrarium, let my Ichor flood you"*), the following was clarified:

- **Ichor is a god's own magic — the domain itself — not a bodily fluid.** [[Sacrarium]] *is* Anima's Ichor. [[Nexum]] *is* Tellus's Ichor. Whatever Aequor channels into Silvia while training her is his.
- **Colors:** Anima = **gold**. Tellus = **silver** (newly assigned this session). Aequor = **teal**.
- **Blood is a separate concept — ordinary life-force — decoupled from Ichor's color.** When a god's blood is shown running one of these colors in a scene, it means that god's Ichor is actively present or winning in that moment, not that blood has a fixed natural color.
- **Nexum's own color (gold) is now explained structurally, not just stated:** an oath is agency (Anima, gold) turned toward duty (Tellus, silver) — the free choice to bind yourself to an obligation. Neither half makes an oath alone. Nexum isn't simply "duty's mirror to Sacrarium's agency," it's built from both, which is why it manifests gold when loosed at Nexus's death: agency visibly dominant, because an oath is at its core an act of choosing.

### The silver/teal cross-motif

This reframes an existing, previously-unexplained detail rather than introducing a new one: Silvia's silver hair/eyes (present from birth, gold-flecked only once the Sacrarium reacts) and Nexus's teal eyes (mirroring Aequor's, also unexplained from birth) turn out to be **each sibling marked by the brother they do not end up bound to** — Silvia carries Tellus's color but is raised close to Aequor; Nexus carries Aequor's color but ends up fused with Tellus. The wiki's prior guess that Silvia's silver marked her as a Sacrarium-carrier was wrong and has been corrected. **This is left as a deliberate, unresolved in-world mystery** — the user was asked whether to give it an in-text explanation and did not choose to when the question came up; default to keeping it unexplained unless raised again.

## 6. The epilogue: how Nexus actually dies

Written directly by the user as a full scene, then reconciled into the sequence above:

- **The promise.** Within Silvia's own lifetime, Tellus's ongoing corrosion of Nexus (see below) becomes visible. Silvia extracts a **passive contract**, not a sworn oath: don't be ruthless, don't lose yourself. As the Fae King and eventual source of the entire Nexum system, Nexus's own stake in this promise is enormous — so is the cost of straining it.
- **The centuries.** Nexus does not spend his reign as a hero. Tellus's identity-unraveling never stopped inside him — it keeps corroding him the entire time. Nexus starts deliberately straining against his promise to Silvia on purpose, using the self-inflicted Nexum backlash as a weapon against the corruption — pain and consequence jolting him back to himself. It works, imperfectly, for a long time. It is not a cure.
- **The request refused.** Nexus eventually asks Silvia to kill him outright, before it gets worse. She can't.
- **The spear.** Unable to grant his request and unwilling to leave him undefended against himself, Silvia spends her final years forging a spear — silver shaft, tipped with a scale taken from Anima himself, gold — the only thing besides a god's own magic capable of wounding one. She gives it to him herself, in her late thirties. Silvia dies afterward, an ordinary mortal death, not otherwise detailed.
- **The war.** Centuries later, a war reaches Nexus's castle. In the fighting, fully ruthless for the first time, he breaks the contract with Silvia completely rather than merely straining it. The backlash is the single largest self-inflicted cost the mechanic has ever produced — not a warning jolt this time, the Fae King's entire stake collapsing at once — and it staggers him badly enough to open him to a blow no ordinary weapon could have landed. An unnamed combatant, a casualty of the same war, drives Silvia's spear into his chest.
- **The mechanism, resolved.** This *is* "the trick that turns a god's own magic against itself" the original plan called for — not a separate oath-based loophole as first speculated, but literally the passive-contract mechanic pushed to its maximum stakes against its own originator. In the wound, Tellus's silver Ichor fights to heal him — pure survival instinct, the same reflex that started all of this — and loses to Anima's gold, carried in the spear. Nexus's blood runs gold as he dies: not his blood's natural color, but Anima's Ichor visibly overtaking his body as it wins.
- **The succession.** Dying, Nexus calls forward his distant descendant Corvinus and compels him into a **formal, active** oath to rule in his place — Corvinus's real reluctance ("how would I outdo the immortal king of honor?") is physically overridden, chains rising from Nexus's own spilled Ichor to bind and crown him. See [[Corvinus]].
- **The release.** Nexus's death frees the fully-unraveled, half-mad Tellus and looses Nexum itself on the world — see [[Nexum]] and [[Tellus]] for how this feeds into the Aetas of Manus and the Malum proper.

## 7. Remaining open threads

1. The Nexum-based misogyny thread — society treating Nexum as sex-locked when it mechanically isn't (no one extends a woman credit on the strength of her word) — flagged as good, deliberately undeveloped dark territory, not yet written into any scene.
2. Whether the silver/teal cross-motif ever gets an in-story explanation, or stays a permanent mystery.
3. The identity of whoever actually killed Nexus with Silvia's spear — deliberately anonymous.
4. Corvinus's reign and character beyond the coronation scene — currently a stub.
5. The nature and cause of the war that reaches Nexus's castle — not yet developed beyond "a war."
