# Nexum

**Type:** Ambient law / bond-magic, settled over the world at the Malum
**Origin:** The residue of the oath [[Tellus]] broke against a five-year-old [[Nexus]] — loosed as general law only centuries later, at the moment Nexus dies and the Tellus he sheltered is set free
**Domain:** Oaths, bonds, obligation — the spoken word made binding

## Description

Where the [[Sacrarium]] is a body carried in blood — a fragment of a god's heart, inherited mother to daughter — Nexum is not carried at all. It is a law the world itself now obeys. The moment two parties speak a bond aloud, it is sealed, whether or not either of them meant it as a vow.

> [!key-insight] Ichor is not blood
> **Ichor** is a god's own magic — the domain itself, not a fluid. Sacrarium *is* [[Anima]]'s Ichor (gold). Nexum *is* [[Tellus]]'s Ichor (silver). Whatever [[Aequor]] carries and channels into Silvia when he trains her (see [[Lore in order]], Ch. 3 — "let my Ichor flood you") is his (teal). **Blood** is a separate thing — ordinary life-force, not fixed to any of these colors on its own. When Nexus's blood turns gold as he dies (see [[Nexus]]), that isn't his blood's natural color; it's Anima's Ichor, carried in the spear that kills him, visibly overtaking everything else in his body as it wins.

*"Hey, let's get coffee — I'm paying." "Bet."* That exchange is now a contract. One party owes coffee. Breaking it invites punishment, automatically, with no ritual, no writing, and no invocation required.

## Origin: The Malum

Nexum's true origin is a single broken promise — but it does not begin as a law. At five years old, [[Nexus]] — second child of [[Vesta]] and [[Primum]], younger brother to [[Silvia]] — argues with [[Tellus]], who had once told him *"I'll protect you, child."* Hot-headed, Tellus tries to physically restrain the boy, and in that one act breaks two things at once, from two different directions:

- **From outside:** restraining Nexus violates the boy's own agency, triggering the dormant Sacrarium residue every son carries (see [[Sacrarium]]) — a defensive reaction lashing out at Tellus.
- **From inside:** Tellus is not merely subject to duty and oath — he is what they are, the god fate drew that gift from before it was ever given to [[Primum]]. Nothing outranks him, so nothing could have restrained him from breaking his own word. But nothing can violate what it *is* and remain whole, either. The moment he breaks his own oath, part of who he is stops being true — an identity unraveling from within, not a punishment inflicted from without.

Neither force alone would have ended him. It is the combination — a sustained, external assault he cannot simply out-endure, layered on an internal collapse he cannot simply stop — that becomes the **first oath-breaking backlash the world has ever felt**, and it rips Tellus apart, long before Nexum exists as a law anyone else is bound by.

Facing that, Tellus doesn't fight it. He shelters his essence inside Nexus — the one place the Sacrarium's own assault cannot follow, since it is the boy's to command, not his to strike with. It is a ditch effort, not a discovered power: nothing else in this world can do this, and nothing does it again — when Tellus is finally killed for good at the Malum, there is no second refuge waiting for him. The fusion is what makes Nexus ageless, the first **Fae King** — and what dooms him to spend centuries as the vessel for a god who is still, slowly, coming apart inside him (see [[Nexus]]).

Centuries later, when Nexus is finally killed (see [[Nexus]] — mechanism still undecided), Tellus is freed — not merely weakened by time, but by an unraveling that never stopped. What sheds from Nexus in that same moment is Nexum itself: the one broken oath that nearly killed a god, now loosed on the whole world as a general law. Freed but half mad, Tellus rampages until [[Aequor]] finally kills him for good, the event the timeline already calls **the Malum**, so total it splits the calendar into Before Malum (BM) and After Malum (AM); see [[Timeline of the Aetas]].

Tellus's gift to [[Primum]] was **duty** — obligation given a shape by fate, the counterpart to [[Anima]]'s gift of agency to [[Vesta]] that became the [[Sacrarium]]. But Nexum is not simply duty's mirror to Sacrarium's agency — it is *built from both*. An oath is agency turned toward duty: the free choice (Anima's domain, gold) to bind yourself to an obligation (Tellus's domain, silver). Neither half alone makes an oath — agency without duty is just a wish, duty without agency is just a leash. This is why Nexum manifests gold at the moment it's loosed from Nexus's death: silver and gold combining, agency visibly dominant because an oath is, at its core, an act of choosing.

> [!note] Not a symmetrical origin, on purpose
> [[Anima]]'s sacrifice was chosen, consenting, dignified. Tellus's fall is temper, panic, and failure. That asymmetry is deliberate, not an oversight — if every god's ending were a noble self-sacrifice, the pattern would read as a formula the story reaches for on demand rather than two different deaths earning two different weights.

Nexus's teal eyes were noted from birth as mirroring [[Aequor]]'s own — see [[Nexus]] — a resemblance still unexplained. Surviving from the Aetas of Creation to the Aetas of Manus, some three thousand years, as the vessel Tellus sheltered in implies Nexus carried something more than an ordinary human span even before any of this. *(Open thread — worth deciding explicitly rather than leaving implicit.)*

## Function

### Passive contracts (spoken, unwritten)

Any verbal agreement between two parties binds automatically. No formal invocation is needed — the world enforces it whether the speakers realize what they've done or not.

- The punishment for breaking a passive contract is comparatively weak.
- Its severity scales with **the breaker's own Nexum**, not the wronged party's — the more you hold, the more it costs you to go back on your word. This is what makes a high-Nexum person "trustworthy": they have too much riding on every promise to break it lightly. A person with little Nexum risks almost nothing by breaking a promise, which is exactly why their word is worth little.
- **Fulfilling** a contract raises the fulfiller's Nexum slightly.
- **Breaking** one costs the breaker a portion of their own Nexum, on top of the punishment itself.

Together this makes Nexum a compounding reputation economy, not a static stat: the reliable keep getting more reliable (rising Nexum → harsher self-inflicted stakes → more reason to keep future promises → further rise), while the unreliable erode what little they have with every broken oath, until their word carries almost no weight — and almost no risk — at all. *(Superseded the earlier draft of this page, which had severity scale with the wronged party's holdings instead — that reading is dropped in favor of this one.)*

**Worked example — the highest stakes this mechanic ever reaches:** [[Silvia]] extracts a passive contract from [[Nexus]] — not to be ruthless, not to lose himself — while she's still alive to ask it of him (see [[Nexus]]). As the Fae King, his own Nexum is effectively the ceiling of the whole system; straining against that promise costs him proportionally. He weaponizes this on purpose for centuries, letting the backlash of nearly breaking it snap him back from [[Tellus]]'s corruption. When he finally breaks it outright, in the battle that kills him, the backlash isn't a warning jolt anymore — it's the single largest self-inflicted cost the mechanic has ever produced, and it's what actually opens him up to the killing blow. The magic that was supposed to keep him whole is what finally puts him down.

### Active contracts (formal, deliberate)

Where a passive contract can be broken — at a cost — a deliberately spoken, formal oath cannot. The moment someone invokes one, Nexum holds them to its exact terms: trying to act against it meets literal resistance, the world itself pushing and restraining the oath-taker's body. An oath can also be sworn to *grant* its own terms rather than merely forbid their violation — "I will feel no pain from this" mutes the pain for as long as the oath's condition holds — trading a binding for a power, not just a promise for a penalty.

This is exactly why an ordinary person cannot break a sworn oath but [[Tellus]] could break his: anyone who carries Nexum is still weaker than the law itself, so Nexum can physically overpower them. Tellus was never subject to Nexum — he predates it, and is what it was drawn from. Nothing could restrain him from breaking his own word. What broke instead was him.

**Worked example:** dying, [[Nexus]] compels his descendant [[Corvinus]] to swear a formal oath to rule in his place. Corvinus doesn't want to — he doesn't believe he can be better than the king dying in front of him — and says so mid-oath. It doesn't matter. The moment the words leave his mouth, Nexum holds him to them: the air thickens, his own king's spilled Ichor rises into physical chains, and Corvinus is compelled to leave his dying king behind and walk out to rule, whether he feels ready or not. His reluctance is real and irrelevant — that's the entire point of the active tier.

## Who carries Nexum, and how much

Unlike the Sacrarium, Nexum is not locked to one sex — anyone who can speak a promise can hold it, gain it, and lose it. But it isn't a flat meritocracy either; it runs on both an inherited baseline and a lifetime of individual conduct:

- **Baseline is hereditary.** Certain families carry high Nexum as a standing trait, the way certain bloodlines carry more Sacrarium than others — an inherited head start into the trust economy, not something earned from zero. This is the seed of a Nexum aristocracy: old houses whose word has mattered, and been kept, for generations.
- **Standing moves with behavior.** On top of that baseline, an individual's own Nexum rises with kept promises and falls with broken ones, so two people born into the same family can drift far apart over a lifetime — one compounding upward into real trustworthiness, the other spiraling down into someone whose word (and stake) means nothing.
- **The Fae** — Nexus's own line or creation as Fae King — plausibly sit at the top of this baseline, the way Vesta's line sits at the top of the Sacrarium.

This gives Nexum the same shape as Sacrarium's own privilege problem, from the opposite direction: Sacrarium calcifies into a bloodline aristocracy of who can create; Nexum calcifies into a reputation aristocracy of who can be trusted — and, practically, of whose broken word actually costs them anything.

> [!gap] Open thread — a discrimination that isn't in the mechanics
> Nexum isn't sex-locked; a woman can hold, earn, and lose it exactly as a man can. But nothing stops the world's *people* from acting as if it were — no one extends a woman credit, a contract, or a loan on the strength of her word, regardless of how much Nexum she actually holds. Worth developing deliberately: not a rule of the magic, but a prejudice riding on top of it, mirroring the Sacrarium's own class-discrimination angle (see [[Sacrarium]]) from the opposite direction.

## Thematic note

Sacrarium is bodily, matrilineal, and tied to childbirth — [[Anima]]'s gift to [[Vesta]], nurture and protection carried in blood. Nexum is behavioral, near-universal, and tied to the spoken word — [[Tellus]]'s gift to [[Primum]], duty loosed on the world by his own broken oath. The founding creation myth already splits the two this way (see [[Lore in order]]): woman given agency to nurture and create, man given duty to keep. Where Sacrarium answers "who can create," Nexum answers "who can be held to their word, and who can hold others to theirs" — a second axis of power in the world that doesn't run through a womb, and doesn't require the man it's centered on to have been its hero. [[Nexus]] doesn't choose any of this; he is closer to its casualty than its architect. The magic is his by circumstance, not by deed.
