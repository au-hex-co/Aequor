# Lore in Order

The canonical, chronological telling of the origin myth: the creation of Primum, the making of Vesta, the birth of Ferus and Silvia, and the founding of the Sacrarium. This supersedes earlier draft fragments. It is the single source of truth for these scenes.

#### Chapter 1: Silvia

~ The Lens focused on Silvia ~

[[Silvia]] stood on the porch of her wood hut. She had just recently turned five, and her younger brother [[Nexus]] was born today, but she wasn't allowed to watch. She was told to stand guard on the porch, so she did. She puffed her chest out and stared at the trees and bushes, daring anyone to jump out. 

Usually she wasn't allowed to leave the house without one of her parents; her father always went out, one hand trailing loose against every stalk and low branch he passed, without ever seeming to notice he was doing it, and her mother preferred to stay home and take care of the house, singing the same wandering, wordless tune she always did while she worked. 

[[Silvia]] had never once heard her start it, only ever caught it already halfway through. When she turned five, her parents tentatively allowed her to go exploring on her own. Little did they know that hadn't stopped her wandering the forest alone even before she was five. But they had nothing to fear; they lived in a safe paradise.

[[Silvia]] stood there for one minute, two minutes, five minutes, before she caved. *It's so boring, why must I stay? I'll head out. It will be fine.* Slowly she walked down the gravel path and reached her spot. There was a small hill in a clearing, and in the middle of the rolling hills stood an even bigger tree. It towered far above the others, reaching high into the clouds.

She craned her little head and swiped her silver hair from her face. She looked up at the towering tree and yelled, "I'm here!"

The second her voice pierced the quiet, the ground started to shake, and the tree in front of her began to shift. A massive branch swept down and unfurled before [[Silvia]] like an open palm.

When [[Silvia]] climbed onto it, the branches slowly and gently curled around her, holding her as they rose, carrying her high up into the sky.

As she stood atop the clouds, [[Silvia]] looked at her home. It was a sprawling forest with a river running through the middle, a mountain range on one side towering so high it made the tree she stood on seem like an ant, and on the other side, an endless blue ocean with waves crashing into the land relentlessly. As she watched the sun slowly set, she saw a glint in the water, then scales flashing past, fast. As soon as she saw it, it was gone.

By the time little [[Silvia]] got home, the lights were out, and she didn't want to disturb her tired mother, so she went to bed.

The next morning, [[Silvia]] stayed home and, for the first time, saw someone other than her parents: her new brother, [[Nexus]]. As she held him and looked into his deep teal eyes, she felt like she was looking into an ocean. She felt joy and kinship. She felt love for him.

[[Silvia]] played with [[Nexus]] until sundown, at which point their mother picked him up for dinner, and together they sat at a sturdy wooden table. The room was lit by wax candles. As the candlelight flickered, [[Vesta]] brought dinner.

When [[Silvia]] woke up, she took her mother and brother to the clearing. It was a short five-minute walk, but halfway through, [[Vesta]], exhausted, turned back for home, leaving the two siblings to finish their trek alone. 

When [[Silvia]] reached the tree with her brother, she climbed to the top while holding him, the tree easing their climb by gently pushing them up. She sat down and pouted. "Why do you only make sounds? I want someone to talk to," she said. 
She always wanted a companion, someone to keep her company. She leaned back into the branches and felt them caress her as she continued, "Mom said you would be able to talk like us, and to give it time. I can't wait!"

As the sun set, she held her brother tightly and whispered, "It's you and me."

---

[[Silvia]] woke to her father gently shaking her awake. "Let's go to the creek!" he said. Suddenly filled with energy, [[Silvia]] jumped up and scampered to her brother's room. She saw her mother lying on the cotton bed, the one it had taken her father a year to make, hugging her baby boy. Not wanting to disturb her, [[Silvia]] slipped out quietly to meet her father on the porch.

His little girl reached the porch, pulling some leather over her feet to protect her toes, and said, "Mum was sleeping. I didn't want to wake her up." Then she ran forward into the misty forest, her father following behind.

Though it was still early, in the first hours of sunrise, the birds and animals made the forest feel alive with chirping and the occasional cry of some exotic bird.

[[Silvia]] and her father walked at an easy, uneven pace; she usually ran ahead, but whenever she had something to say, she'd slow down and let her father catch up. Finally, as she ran, she spotted a red flower with petals hugging each other. She'd seen such flowers before and named them roses, but this time, there was water beaded on the green leaves of its thorny stem. Confused, she turned back to her father and asked, "Pa, it didn't rain, why is water on this flower?"

Her father laughed. "That's dew. Every morning, the air itself cries for [[Anima]], leaving dew on the leaves as a mark of His existence."

[[Silvia]], even more confused now, asked, "Pa, who is [[Anima]]? Why does the air cry for him?"

[[Primum]] sighed. "He is the most high. He sacrificed Himself for our freedom. I'll tell you more when you're of age." 

"Why not now?" [[Silvia]] complained as she stomped ahead.

When [[Silvia]] looked up from the rose, she realized the forest had gone entirely quiet. Eerily quiet. As they walked, the silence held, broken only by the rushing of the creek. Though [[Silvia]] and her father called it "the creek," it spanned a vast section, home to hundreds of fish.

When [[Primum]] reached its edge, sensing a teaching moment, he turned to his daughter. "On the edge of a river is a bank, so right now, I'm standing on a bank. But the edge of the sea is called a shore." [[Silvia]], intrigued, repeated the words in halting syllables: "Sh...o...re. Ba...enk." They felt strange in her mouth, but right, somehow, all the same.

As they walked up the bank, they saw a footprint alongside a handprint, like someone had crawled past. The moment [[Primum]] saw it, he went quiet, and they returned home without fishing. But [[Silvia]] wasn't bothered; they were in a **Safe** paradise.

The next day, [[Silvia]] played with [[Nexus]] and carried him everywhere. The following morning, however, she was so tired from playing that she stayed asleep long past midday. When she finally woke up, the house was quiet. No one was home. Feeling lonely, she went to her spot, her tree. When she reached the top, she looked out to the sea, searching for that glint of the beast moving through the water.

When she got home around midnight, she slowly entered the bedroom. There was a crate lined with cotton fluff, nesting a small child who was sleeping soundly, and beside it the cotton bed, where she could see the outline of her parents in the moonlight, intertwined together. Not wanting to disturb them, she slowly left and walked out of the house. She wasn't sleepy at all, so she decided she would go look for that beast. Happily trotting, [[Silvia]] headed for the sea.

As [[Silvia]] approached the shore, she felt something weird against her feet. It was solid, but it kept slipping and running like water, running between her toes as her small feet slowly sank. Despite the strange shifting ground, she kept moving towards the deep blue ocean, trying to find that scaly beast she had seen before.

She plopped down onto the rock-water and waited, watching fish swim and small birds chirp and flit across the sky, but she was looking for that great scaled monster.

Slowly the night passed, and [[Silvia]] still stood at the water's edge. She did not want to miss the chance to glance at that magnificent beast again, but as she waited, she heard a growl behind her. She sprang to her little feet and spun around, and saw... it looked like her father, but it wasn't. It walked on its hands and feet. It slowly moved closer, but she stood rooted in fear. What was it? Why did it look like her father? The more she looked at the beast, the more she saw: deep brown eyes, thick and dirty limbs. Looking at it, she felt something weird.

As Silvia watched, the beast circled her slowly, its eyes keen. Scared but still curious, she spoke softly, "Hello, my name is [[Silvia]]." As she stood on the shore watching the beast, she felt a soft sucking on her feet. She looked down to see the water rushing back into the ocean. However, the second she broke eye contact, the beast took its chance.

The beast leapt towards her, reaching its hands out. It grabbed [[Silvia]]'s throat and pushed her hard against the rock-water. She felt the breath leave her lungs. She tried to yell, but she couldn't. She needed air. She scratched at the arms holding her throat, clawing for the sweet air that was right outside her reach, but it was to no avail. She spread her hands out trying to push her assailant off, but she was too weak. She grabbed some rock-water and flung it at the beast, but it barely flinched.

Just as [[Silvia]] closed her eyes, all sound seemed to vacate the area. She wasn't sure if it was because she was losing consciousness, but she found it strangely peaceful, and then it was interrupted.

[[Silvia]] opened her eyes to see a brilliant gold light filling the bank as the beast burned. Tiny, translucent gold lashes left trails of blood along the beast's back, and as it looked at the tiny girl in its hands, it saw dazzling golden eyes staring back.

But as quickly as it appeared, the light disappeared. The beast backed off, still watching, still circling her, when they both felt a warning: *something's coming*.

The roar was deafening. It wasn't terrifying, but rather mind-numbing. As the dragon flew out of the water and approached, [[Silvia]] felt the ground tremble and the chirping birds fall silent. The beast let go of her and ran back into the forest. [[Silvia]] felt the air rush back into her lungs, filling her with breath. She was relieved for a second, before turning around to be face to face with a massive eye.


#### Chapter 2: Aequor

[[Silvia]] tried to scream as she fell back, but her throat closed up and her body refused to listen to her. She felt rooted like the trees she loved as she watched the beast. [[Silvia]] couldn't run. Couldn't call for help. Regretting her decision to come looking for the dragon in front of her, she waited. A single tear fell down her cheek.

The whole world seemed to freeze as they looked at one another. The tide that had once retreated to herald **H**is arrival came crashing back all the way up to her ankles, relentless, like the dragon in front of her.
[[Aequor]] saw a small pitiful girl with silver eyes and softly said,
"You have nothing to fear from me, little one. I mean no harm to you."
[[Silvia]], rooted in the spot, heard a voice, but it didn't come from the dragon; it was like the ground she stood on was talking, like the air around her whispered into her ear. 
She watched as the beautiful, scaled dragon circled her the way the beast had, before settling with its eye in front of her again.

"Who are you?" [[Silvia]] stammered.

The dragon rested his head on the sea bank and looked at [[Silvia]]. Just as the dragon was about to speak, he saw a glint of gold in her eyes. This majestic beast looked shocked before collecting himself. "[[Aequor]], you can call me [[Aequor]]."

[[Silvia]] looked at the majestic beast and saw the blue scales outlined with white and an ancient face with two deep-set eyes on either side. She kept backing away until she bumped into the dragon's tail, and fell to the ground in shock.
[[Aequor]] looked at the small stunned child and said, "You are safe now, child. However, if you are ever in danger, say my name and I will help you. Now, child, return home."


"Wait!" [[Silvia]] tried to yell, hands at her throat, as she watched the dragon defy the ground, fly up, and dive into the water. Scared from the experience, she ran home.

[[Vesta]] woke up early to find her daughter missing, but wasn't concerned, for nothing could hurt them on this **safe** haven. When she saw her daughter run up to the house holding her neck, she ran outside and asked her child, "Silvie, Silvie, what happened, dear girl?"

[[Silvia]], terrified of the beast, cried but didn't respond. [[Vesta]] didn't know what to feel, since she didn't know what **could** happen. She tried probing for what happened, but [[Silvia]] just lay in her mother's arms. Finally, not wanting to go back to being escorted everywhere, she softly said, "I fell."

When [[Vesta]] tried talking to [[Primum]] about what happened to [[Silvia]], he didn't seem to think much of it, for this island was safe.

The next day, [[Silvia]] first ran to her spot. She felt scared; suddenly this home that had always been **safe** didn't feel that way anymore, so she went to the one place that still did. As she approached her spot, she saw the familiar clearing with the towering tree. She ran to the tree and yelled in joy, "Sir Tree, Sir Tree, you won't believe what happened to me!" She felt at ease again. Mr. Tree would undoubtedly protect her.

Although the tree could not talk, Silvia imagined it did. She considered the tree her best friend in the whole world. She, however, could not settle on a name for the tree. It was between Cara and Vera. Her mother, though, did once suggest Amica when a four-year-old [[Silvia]] asked for her opinion. That, however, was long lost to history.

As [[Silvia]] hugged Mr. Tree and he hugged back, the branches finally eased away, letting her tell him the story...
As the trees swayed, Silvia told the story of how she reached the shore, saw the rock-water, and finally, how the dragon saved her from the beast.
"And his name is..." she said, before pausing. *He told me to only say his name if I'm in danger.* The tree gently shook a warning before settling.

[[Silvia]] spent all day on Mr. Tree and looked out to the sea, wondering if she would see the dragon again. While she was returning home, she saw her father and ran to him. [[Primum]], who had just come back from the woods, looked up and saw a bundle of silver run toward him and into his arms. He hoisted her up and carried her inside for dinner as a family.

The following day, [[Silvia]]'s father disappeared again like he always did, but today Silvia had a plan. She grabbed her little brother and took him to the sea. As she stood at the shore, she sat on the rock-water again, but this time she would not wait until dark; she was still scared of the beast. As dusk approached, she finally gave up on seeing [[Aequor]] and returned home. Alas, [[Silvia]] did not give up. Every day, as soon as her father left, she would walk the shore seeking the dragon, some days with her brother, some without. One day she even brought her mother, but it was to no avail; the water kept smashing against the shore.

Three days had passed since she'd seen the dragon. [[Silvia]] was starting to lose hope of seeing him again, and she was planning to stay home and play with her brother, but then she heard her parents in their room, talking to each other. She stood by the door, hearing the muffled sounds, but she could not make out the words. Eventually, bored, she grabbed her crying brother and left for the shore. As she approached the shore, the wind started picking up.

[[Silvia]], as usual, started sitting and playing with her brother when she felt a slight tremble, almost a warning, before seeing the shoreline recede like water running away. She excitedly got up, waiting to see a dragon, but nothing. *The dragon didn't come?* she thought, as she continued to play with her brother, building the little prince a big castle.

The sand beneath her hands stilled, and the air itself seemed to lean close, ancient, and patient.

"What are you building?"

[[Silvia]] heard the voice inside her bones and, full of excitement again, turned to look at the dragon. She was not disappointed: she saw the beautiful teal scales and long whiskers as the dragon lay in the sand, its giant eye open, watching her from the side. Although [[Silvia]] had planned this day for ages, she was left speechless at the sight of [[Aequor]]. When she finally found her voice, she looked up and said, "[[Aequor]], [[Aequor]], look, I brought my brother!" as she proudly presented the small infant to the eye.

[[Aequor]] looked at the smaller child and saw teal eyes staring back. **H**e studied the infant, whose eyes never left his; two teal gazes interlocked. [[Nexus]], who had been crying, stopped and looked at the ocean-blue eye. Finally, [[Aequor]] looked back to [[Silvia]] and replied, "He does not carry it. I can feel its shape near him... and yet it is not his."

[[Silvia]], confused, just stared at the dragon's eye before hugging her baby brother. Concerned, she asked, "What does he not have? How can I get it for him?"

[[Aequor]]'s eyes softened and asked, "What's your name, child?" 

Blankly staring at him, she finally responded, "[[Silvia]]."

[[Aequor]] spoke again, "How about this, child of the wildwood? I will tell you my story, and you tell me yours."

[[Silvia]] looked surprised and finally responded, "Your story?"

### Chapter 3: The Three
And so [[Silvia]] sat listening to [[Aequor]] tell his story:

"Once upon a time.
When the world was brought forth, there were three brothers. One was made from the dust of the land, [[Tellus]], unyielding as the earth itself. One was made from the water of the oceans, [[Aequor]], deep and wise as the depths he came from. And the last was made from the wisps of the air, [[Anima]], who moved as freely as the wind that made him."

"Was that you, [[Aequor]], or was it a different [[Aequor]]?" [[Silvia]] interrupted, before instantly going quiet as [[Aequor]]'s distant eyes locked onto hers. He slowly said, "Perhaps."

The Three lived on Terra for 108 years before one, bored, looked at the others and asked, "I sure am bored, how about you both?" [[Aequor]] and [[Tellus]] both agreed. [[Tellus]] had created mountains and caverns, but it had all been for nothing. [[Aequor]] had made trenches, reefs, and lakes, but it all felt... bland.

So the Three collectively agreed on making life. One species for each brother. [[Tellus]] started off: he pulled a root from a great tree, carved eyes into it, and gave it the name Serpent. The snake came alive and looked at the Three and asked, "What is my duty?" [[Tellus]] looked proud.

"But how can a snake talk?" [[Silvia]] burst out. She had seen plenty of snakes: they kept to themselves and never bothered her, but she'd never heard one talk. [[Aequor]], taken aback, said, "Ah, child, those are snakes. Perhaps Serpent is a more fitting name for this one; it was, after all, the first creation."

Alas, the others, too, thought it didn't fit their idea of a good creation, so [[Aequor]] grabbed a great rock from the shore and gave it fins. "This is a whale. A magnificent creature." But [[Anima]] saw its mechanical movements and said, "That's no creature, that is a machine!" He, too, grabbed the clouds, shaped fire, and formed it into a lion's mane with golden wings. "I call this a griffin!" he said, as the majestic beast soared through the sky.

Over the next 100 years they each took turns making creatures, each more bizarre and unique, but none that felt right. Each one fell short of a criterion the brothers couldn't quite name.

In the 208th year of creation, [[Anima]] suggested something radical: to each provide one aspect of a new creature. So [[Tellus]] made a mold with mud, shaping a human with a face, eyes, and more, but it would not hold its shape: it kept slipping. So [[Aequor]] provided water for the mud to float in and hold together, but when it moved, it moved without reason, so [[Anima]] breathed freedom into the clay figure and gave it agency.

Together they named him [[Primum]].

This was the mark of a new era.

When [[Aequor]] finished the story, he looked at the two children, who stared back at him, wanting more. As his eyes softened, he told [[Silvia]], "Let's meet here every 7th day. I will tell you my story and train you. In turn, you tell me yours, how your week went."

[[Silvia]] nodded excitedly before getting hung up on one word, "Train?" she asked.

[[Aequor]] nodded his giant head, sending the nearby ground flying, before leaping back into the water and leaving without another word.

Week by week, little [[Silvia]] visited [[Aequor]], never missing a single one. For who would miss a visit from a dragon of creation?

The first week she went, [[Aequor]] didn't do any training, but rather set up some ground rules. "I will only train you after you turn 7, and my stories will be earned, not given: prove yourself through combat."

[[Silvia]], confused, asked, "[[Aequor]], if I don't train, how can I hear your story?"

The dragon gently rested his head on the ground and said, "Come here, little one, use my scales as holds and climb up. Do not assume that all combat is physical. I will teach you the ways of this world. I will show you the truth."

[[Silvia]] cautiously walked up to the dragon's head and touched a scale softly.

The second she touched the scale, she felt it before she saw it: a surge of teal flooding the scale and crashing toward her arm. Before she could yank her hand back, a brilliant golden light filled her veins. Her arm lit up with golden lines where it touched [[Aequor]], rising to meet the teal flood... and the two crashed together. 

Slowly she pulled herself onto the scale, wedging her foot into the cracks between the plates. She hoisted herself the rest of the way and sat in the middle of one of the ridges along the dragon's spine.

As she sat, she felt something within her tingle, spreading to every point where her skin touched the dragon, at each one a complicated golden symbol flared to life and held there, like a ward. She didn't know what it meant, so she just sat admiring the dazzling glow, until she felt the ground beneath her give way as they both flew up into the sky.

[[Aequor]], who felt the child on him settle, finally spoke, "Let go of your [[Sacrarium]]; let my Ichor flood you."

[[Silvia]], who had never heard of either, just sat confused and asked, "How do I do that?"

"Close your eyes. What do you see?" [[Aequor]] asked.

[[Silvia]] closed her eyes and saw nothing. "Focus on your stomach. Imagine a fire," [[Aequor]] continued.

As she followed his instructions, she pictured a fire burning within her, bright, and orange. Suddenly, the fire started shifting. In front of [[Silvia]]'s eyes, it turned golden and then shifted into a golden fragment that seemed to have rivers of brilliant golden light flowing out, filling her whole body. She described what happened to [[Aequor]], who then said, "Cover the fragment; hold it tight."

Obedient as ever, [[Silvia]] didn't question the dragon. She held the fragment within her palm and squeezed, and felt the golden glow leave her body as it returned to the fragment.

When the last wisps of gold left her, she felt a cold wave flood her, freezing every part of her in place. When she opened her eyes, she saw symbols everywhere. Looking at the scales, she felt dizzy at the sheer amount of information. Though [[Silvia]] had never seen these symbols before, just by looking at them she intimately knew how they worked; she could see them in the wind, the clouds, in everything.

When she looked up, she saw a single massive eye with a complicated symbol at its center, and the moment she met its gaze, she passed out.

### Chapter Unknown: Maiden of the Fog

[[Primum]], though he had agency, had no knowledge and did not know how to move his own body. This disappointed the Three. So [[Aequor]], brother of wisdom, filled [[Primum]] with wisdom and resolve, and decreed it so: the older [[Primum]] and his children grew, the wiser and steadfast they would become. Then [[Tellus]], brother of the land, laid a hand on [[Primum]]'s chest and gave him duty, and with duty, gave fate a shape to his life that was owed to something beyond himself.

Now able to move and talk, [[Primum]] looked at the brothers, his creators, and asked, "Oh lords, you have given me freedom and wisdom. I can do what I like, I know how to do them, but fathers, what is my purpose?"

This same question had already been asked by the snake, the first creation. The Three looked at each other, clueless. For what purpose did they make [[Primum]]? What was his job?

Finally, [[Tellus]] spoke, loud and clear, "[[Primum]], you were made to entertain and keep us company on this lush planet."

[[Primum]] seemed to brighten. He had a tangible purpose, but he still seemed a little sad, so [[Anima]] asked, "[[Primum]], what makes you so sad? Surely freedom is worth more than the shackles of duty? You do not have to fulfill your purpose, you are free!"

To that, [[Primum]] looked at [[Anima]] and simply stated, "For if I am to entertain you, who is to entertain me? Who is to keep me company? If I do not entertain you, what am I worth to you?"

The Three looked stunned once again. In the 208 years they had traversed this land, they had never faced this question. They spent over 100 years making [[Anima]]ls, for what? What were they trying to achieve?

Finally, [[Aequor]] spoke, "Ah, [[Primum]], you ask questions from us that we cannot answer, but I will say to you: your worth is determined by you alone."

And [[Tellus]] quickly followed up, "And on account of your boredom, we will make you a partner, one that you can share company with, but remember! A price is always exacted for what fate bestows."

[[Primum]] stood still for a minute, thinking, and finally he spoke. "I will accept any price you exact."

The Three came together again, but this time [[Tellus]] did not reach for mud. He reached for [[Primum]]. Without warning, he tore the flesh from [[Primum]]'s left arm and the bone from his right leg, and [[Primum]] collapsed to the ground screaming in an agony the world had never felt before, for nothing had ever been made to feel pain until now.

The Three did not stop. [[Tellus]] pressed the torn flesh into a new shape, [[Aequor]] poured water through it until it held, and [[Anima]] bent low and breathed into it the same breath he had given [[Primum]]. Where [[Primum]] had been shaped whole from the dust of [[Tellus]], this new creation was shaped from [[Primum]] himself. His agony became her flesh, his wholeness the price paid for her. When she opened her eyes, the Three named her [[Vesta]].

[[Primum]] watched, still bleeding, and understood at last what [[Aequor]] had meant by resolve, for in the end [[Vesta]] was worth it.

Over the next 20 years, both [[Primum]] and [[Vesta]] came together in union, and separate from that, [[Primum]] entertained the Three and kept them company while [[Vesta]] took care of the home [[Primum]] built.

On the first day of the 21st year, [[Primum]] asked, "Oh fathers, I have given you company for 20 years now. But I too wish to experience fatherhood, I too wish to pass on a legacy. Unlike you, I will surely die, and when I do, I wish to leave a mark in this world I call home."

The Three looked at each other. How could a creation create? they thought.

[[Tellus]] then spoke up and said, "You have got it wrong, child. We too will die. All that lives must die. That is the fate of this world."

[[Primum]] was shocked. He looked at [[Tellus]] and asked, confused, "Are you not gods? Are you not immortal? Who made these rules?"

[[Anima]] looked sheepishly at [[Aequor]], who was stoic, while [[Tellus]] grew angry at his creation for the first time. "Who are you to question us, child? Did we not fulfill your wish? Did we not give you what you desired? For the truth is, we are here; we don't know how or why. All we know is we woke up on this desolate planet and made it into what you see today!"

[[Primum]] looked taken aback. The brothers had always treated him with nothing but love until today. Today he felt the rage of the Three for the very first time. Shocked, he turned and ran back to his home.

Once [[Primum]] left, [[Tellus]] stood on his mountain, still boiling with rage. "How could a mere... mere human question US?" he yelled. [[Aequor]] had already retreated into the sea, letting [[Tellus]] vent his rage, but [[Anima]] stood with him. "Would you not do the same?" he asked innocently.

That took [[Tellus]] aback. For all he had experienced, he had never before put himself in the position of his creation, and he was conflicted. To calm down, he shoved all his emotion into the land around him, filling it with molten rock.

"Perhaps we should give them fertility?" [[Anima]] remarked as he watched [[Tellus]] reflect on himself.

[[Tellus]] nodded, and the Three visited [[Primum]].

"You know the price for life?" [[Tellus]] asked. [[Primum]] looked at [[Tellus]] with fear, his missing arm and leg still throbbing, but [[Tellus]] wasn't looking at [[Primum]]; he was looking at [[Vesta]].

[[Vesta]] looked shocked as she watched the brother of earth bow, his great eye gazing at her directly.

"Y... yy... yes," [[Vesta]] stammered. [[Tellus]] then backed off, and the brothers talked among themselves, for they did not have the domain of fertility. How could it be granted?

[[Tellus]] then reached into the tree closest to him, took out an apple, and gave it to [[Primum]]. "Eat this and come together as one. You should bear children."

So they did just that. [[Primum]] and [[Vesta]] coupled, and the next day [[Vesta]] did not feel anything, but she trusted the brothers; she trusted [[Primum]]. Over the next 8 days they went about as normal, [[Primum]] entertaining the Three, [[Vesta]] taking care of the house, but on the 9th day it all changed.

[[Vesta]] felt pressure in her abdomen. [[Primum]] was still out; she was alone. As the pressure increased, she collapsed to the floor in pain, crawling toward the door; she needed air, fresh air.

With every gasp she pulled herself closer to that ever-distant door and finally pushed it open with all her might, and... a flesh-rending scream tore out of [[Vesta]]'s throat as her flesh ripped.

While [[Vesta]] lay on the ground struggling to move, to breathe, a small vine crawled along the ground, and all the trees seemed to lean over to cover [[Vesta]] from the harsh sun. The vines slowly crawled to [[Vesta]] and caressed her.

When [[Vesta]] woke up next, it was sunrise and she was in bed. Her head slowly filled with memories and with agony as she remembered yesterday. She got up immediately and felt herself over, examining all the places that hurt, but there was not a single scratch. She walked outside and saw [[Primum]] sitting, waving a stick in front of a small creature.

The next day, [[Vesta]] walked outside the house and saw the trees that shaded her and helped her. As she walked up, the trees seemed to shake softly, welcoming the maiden of fertility.

As the months passed, the creature grew, and together they named it [[Ferus]]. But as time passed, both [[Primum]] and [[Vesta]] noticed something: [[Ferus]] did not know how to talk. It could only crawl around the ground, working on pure instinct; the child lacked agency.

The next time [[Primum]] visited the gods, he asked the brother of wind, "Oh father that breathed air into my lungs, oh father that gave me free will, my child that you all have graced me with lacks that which you gave me. Oh fathers, I beg that you give my children the agency and freedom you have granted me."

[[Anima]], one of the most loving brothers, was stunned. The request was so heartfelt, so vulnerable, but agency could only be given to each child one at a time.

[[Tellus]] and [[Aequor]] both looked at [[Anima]], who had no idea how he could grant the child the ability to give agency. "Son of mine, I have to give this thought. Give me until the moon covers the sun, and I will give you what you seek," [[Anima]] responded.

"Oh thank you, father!" [[Primum]] yelled, overjoyed, and ran back to tell his [[Vesta]].

[[Anima]] stood in his magnificent kingdom, looking at all that he had built. The clouds formed a castle, his bastion. Alas, when he was asked an impossible question, [[Anima]] made the hardest choice.

[[Tellus]] and [[Aequor]] both came to the kingdom of fog and saw what [[Anima]] was planning. Both understood why but were sad nonetheless. [[Anima]] looked sadly at his brothers, then looked down on all of creation, and smiled. "A price is always exacted, right, [[Tellus]]?" [[Anima]] looked at the chains that each had brought. Slowly but surely, [[Anima]] placed himself in the chains, and just before his kingdom was sealed, [[Aequor]] apologized to [[Anima]] before thrusting a talon into [[Anima]] and ripping out his heart.

As soon as his heart was ripped out, [[Anima]] went rabid, his mind unable to hold the pressure of the pain. He collapsed, and the golden light in his eye faded. [[Tellus]] and [[Aequor]] both backed out of the castle, and the castle doors closed, sealing themselves off from the world.

The Two came side by side up to the house where [[Vesta]] and [[Primum]] lived. When they knocked, [[Primum]] ran to the door to welcome them, but when he saw only two, [[Primum]] asked, "Oh fathers, you grace me with your visit, but if I may ask, where is the father of the wind?"

[[Tellus]] smiled sadly and said, "Oh child, for the father loved you so, he gave his freedom so your wish may come true. Now, let us see [[Vesta]]."

[[Primum]] heard the words but didn't understand what he meant, for what was freedom to a god?

When [[Vesta]] saw the Two coming towards her, she prostrated herself. "Oh fathers, why do you grace me with your presence?" she asked.

"Rejoice, child, for your wish will come true, but all wishes come with a price," [[Aequor]] said.

[[Vesta]] did not want this wish, for after all, the pain that it brought last time was immense, but she could not refuse a god, so she accepted a round, smooth ball that weighed like a feather.

"Eat this, and your children from henceforth shall breathe of their own volition," [[Tellus]] told [[Vesta]].

"Oh father, I doubt you not, but if I may ask, what is it you wish for me to eat?" [[Vesta]] asked, with as much respect as she could manage. [[Tellus]] just looked at the orb, saw a golden glint, and smiled, giving a one-word answer: "[[Sacrarium]]."

Three days after the Two left, [[Vesta]] was finally convinced by [[Primum]] and ate the orb. When she did, she felt a breeze rustle through her hair, and heard whispers on the wind. That night when they coupled, it was different.

The next 10 days were a confusing period for [[Vesta]]. Instead of birthing the new child, she simply craved more food. They coupled nearly every night, even when [[Vesta]] didn't feel like it; [[Primum]] did not give up on having a child.

Two months after, [[Vesta]] could feel something in her abdomen. It was... alive!

Nine months after, [[Vesta]] lay heavily on her bed. [[Primum]] had once again left to entertain the two remaining gods. [[Vesta]] never did find out where [[Anima]] went, but that was neither here nor there. On the fateful day, [[Vesta]] felt her child would come in the next hour.

[[Vesta]] braced for the pain that would come, but just as it took 30 times longer for the baby to develop, the pain, too, was amplified.

A ripping sound came from [[Vesta]] as her lower abdomen was torn open. The blinding pain filled her whole being. She gripped the blankets tight, but it wasn't enough. She yelled out into the forest, "HELP!" But there was no one to hear her call, except for the trees.

There was a rumbling as [[Vesta]] clawed at herself, trying to ease the pain that made her mind go black. The roof of the house was ripped off, and sunlight flooded in before being blocked by the trees. Roots came up through the house and held [[Vesta]]; this time, one root reached out and touched her mind.

As she yelled, she gave the tree closest to her the ability to speak, the ability to think. She didn't realize or understand what she had done.

"Oh Maiden of the fog, rest, for when you wake, the pain you feel we will remember in your place. Oh Mother of the forest, rest, for now you create life."
