# The Malum: Nexus, Tellus, and the Origin of Nexum

## Context

Aequor's lore currently reads (per an outside reader's critique) as though men aren't structurally needed — reproduction and protection both run through Vesta's line and the Sacrarium, and men can't hold it at all. In response, the last two sessions built **Nexum**, a second magic system (oath/bond magic) that gives men a cosmological anchor of equal weight, tied to Nexus (Silvia's younger brother) becoming the first Fae King. This plan captures the specific origin scene for that transformation — how Nexus becomes Fae King, how Tellus actually dies (the event the wiki already calls **the Malum**, currently dated Year 0 on the Timeline), and how Nexum itself is born — worked out collaboratively this session and confirmed via clarifying questions.

This is a **book/lore planning session, not a code task** — the content below is narrative to be written into the Obsidian wiki (`Aequor/wiki/...`), not software.

## The confirmed sequence

1. **The oath.** At some point before the incident (Nexus is 5), Tellus tells young Nexus something like *"I'll protect you, child."* — a spoken vow.
2. **The argument.** Hot-headed Tellus and 5-year-old Nexus argue. Tellus loses his temper and moves to physically restrain the boy.
3. **Two forces trigger at once, compounding:**
   - Restraining Nexus violates *his own agency* — this triggers the dormant Sacrarium/Anima's-residue reaction sons carry (already-established canon: reacts only in self-defense, when *that child's own* agency is interfered with).
   - Restraining him also **breaks Tellus's own spoken oath** to protect him — the first instance, anywhere in the world, of an oath-breaking backlash. This predates Nexum's formal existence; it's the seed event, not an application of an already-existing law.
   - The two forces compound into something far more violent than an ordinary Sacrarium burn: the backlash **rips Tellus apart**.
4. **The ditch effort.** Facing something no living thing can survive as-is, Tellus doesn't fight the backlash — his survival instinct takes over, and he **shelters/fuses his essence inside Nexus** rather than dying outright.
5. **Fae King Nexus.** This fusion is what makes Nexus ageless/undying by natural means — the first Fae King. Per the user's framing: *"all things must end... but there isn't anything mortal that can kill a god — it would need clever wordplay/trickery, turning the god's own magic against itself."* Nexus is not invincible, just outside ordinary death — consistent with carrying a god's essence.
6. **The long gap.** Centuries pass — this is the bridge across the ~3000 years between the Aetas of Creation (Nexus's birth) and the Aetas of Manus, already on [[Timeline of the Aetas]].
7. **Nexus's death.** Eventually Nexus is killed — by what amounts to a trick that turns a god's own magic on itself, not brute force. **Mechanism intentionally left open** (per user: leave for now) — but flagged as a strong candidate for later: something oath/Nexum-flavored, since that's the one system explicitly born from this exact kind of self-defeating magic.
8. **Tellus is freed — half mad.** Nexus's death releases the weakened, centuries-bound Tellus back into the world. He doesn't come back merely weakened — he comes back **half mad**, specifically because he is the god of oath and duty, and he is the one who broke his own oath ("I'll protect you, child") to cause all of this. The violation of his own domain, not just the physical backlash, is what fractures him. This kicks off a chaos period.
9. **Nexum is loosed.** What sheds from Nexus at the moment of his death is Nexum itself — the private oath-backlash that nearly killed Tellus the first time becomes a general law over the whole world, not just a one-off between two people.
10. **The age of disaster.** Freed, ravaged, and half mad, Tellus rampages until he is finally killed for good — this is the event the Timeline already pins as **Year 0 / "the Malum."** The **Aetas of Manus (541–0 BM)** becomes, retroactively, this disaster period: it starts at Nexus's death and ends when Tellus is finally killed.
11. **Aequor lands the final blow.** Confirmed: it's Aequor who kills Tellus for good, ending the age of disaster — the same role he already played once before, when he was the one who tore out Anima's heart at Anima's own request (Chapter 4, [[Lore in order]]). This time it's final: Tellus is killed **forever**, no fusion/refuge escape available to him a second time. This reframes Anima's earlier fate too — Anima was never confirmed dead in the existing text, only heartless, "rabid," sealed away with his kingdom of clouds. Read consistently with this new beat, Anima is **maimed, not killed** — broken and sealed, but Tellus is the one who actually dies for good. Aequor ends up the one who has now maimed one brother and killed the other, by his own hand, both times.

## Why this holds together with existing canon

- Doesn't contradict the Timeline — it explains *why* Aetas of Manus is 541 years long (a disaster age, not an instant), while Year 0 stays pinned to Tellus's actual death exactly as already written.
- Reuses the already-established sons'-Sacrarium-residue mechanic (self-defense trigger on the child's own agency) rather than inventing a new power.
- Makes Nexum's core rule (breaking an oath costs the breaker, scaled to their own stake) *bootstrap from its own origin story* — the first oath ever broken in this world is Tellus's, and it nearly kills him. The law he becomes subject to is the law his own death eventually seeds into the world.
- Keeps "a price is always exacted" (the throughline already stated on Cosmology.md) intact: Tellus survives his own broken oath by hiding inside a child — and that exact refuge is what makes it possible to finally kill him, centuries later.
- Aequor as final executioner doesn't contradict anything on his existing page (Cosmology.md notes "Aequor alone has no confirmed death or fate in the source material" — this doesn't give him one, it gives him a second act of violence against a brother, on top of the Anima scene already in [[Lore in order]] Chapter 4) and it deepens him rather than adding an unrelated new plot thread — he's cast, deliberately, as the one who ends both his brothers.
- Reframing Anima as maimed-not-dead doesn't contradict his existing page either — it was already ambiguous ("the golden light in his eye faded," kingdom sealed, never stated as death) and this just resolves that ambiguity explicitly rather than retconning it.
