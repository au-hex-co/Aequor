    World/Nexus

    Nexus

    worlddevelopingupdated 2026-09-08#world#character#fae#malum

    Second child of Vesta and Primum, younger brother of Silvia by five years. Born with deep teal eyes — the same shade Silvia later sees again in Aequor, a resemblance Aequor himself reacts to on first meeting her.

    Carried everywhere by Silvia as an infant; the two are inseparable from his birth ("It's you and me," she tells him the night he's born). He is present, still a baby, when Silvia is attacked at the shore and first meets Aequor — she brings him along on later visits to introduce him.

    Fate: The Fae King

    At five years old, Nexus is already carrying the dormant residue of Anima's sacrifice that every son of Vesta's line carries unnoticed — inert unless his own agency is directly interfered with (see Sacrarium). Tellus had told him, at some point before this, "I'll protect you, child."

    The two argue. Hot-headed as ever, Tellus loses his temper and moves to physically restrain the boy — and in that single act breaks two things at once. He violates Nexus's own agency, triggering the residue's defensive reaction. And he breaks his own spoken oath to protect him — the first oath-breaking backlash the world has ever seen, before any such law formally exists. The two forces don't cancel or compete; they compound, and the backlash rips Tellus apart.

    Facing something no living thing survives as-is, Tellus doesn't fight it. His survival instinct takes over, and rather than dying outright he shelters his essence inside Nexus — a last-ditch fusion, not a deliberate binding. It is this fusion, not the killing blow itself, that makes Nexus ageless: the first Fae King, carrying a god inside him, undying by natural means though not invincible — "all things must end... but there isn't anything mortal that can kill a god — it would need clever wordplay, trickery, turning the god's own magic against itself."

    Centuries pass — the long bridge between the Aetas of Creation, when Nexus is born, and the Aetas of Manus (541–0 BM), see Timeline of the Aetas. He does not spend them as a hero quietly waiting his turn. The god fused into him never stopped unraveling from the moment of the backlash, and that unraveling doesn't stay contained to Tellus alone — carrying a god's coming-apart identity for centuries corrodes the boy sheltering him too. Nexus is not the architect of any of this, only its longest casualty: a means to an end, a poor soul slowly corrupted by the mad dragon he carries rather than the king who tamed him.

    Within Silvia's own lifetime, the corrosion is already visible enough that she extracts a promise from him — not a sworn oath, a passive contract, spoken plainly: don't be ruthless, don't lose himself. As Fae King, the source the entire law of Nexum will one day be drawn from, his own stake in that promise is enormous — which means straining against it costs him enormously too. He starts using that cost on purpose: deliberately pushing to the edge of breaking his word to Silvia, letting the self-inflicted backlash jolt him back to himself, fighting Tellus's corruption with the same mechanic that will eventually kill him. It works, for a while. It is not a cure.

    Eventually he asks Silvia to end him outright, before it gets worse. She can't. Unable to grant that and unwilling to leave him undefended against himself, she spends her final years crafting the one weapon that could — a spear, silver-shafted, tipped with a scale taken from Anima himself, gold — and gives it to him with her own hands, in her late thirties. It is a gift and a warning at once: the only thing besides his own magic that could ever wound him, made and given by the one person who couldn't bring herself to use it.

    Silvia dies, in the ordinary way mortals do. Nexus carries both the promise and the spear alone for the centuries that follow, still straining, still using the backlash to hold himself together, the corruption never fully winning but never fully losing either — until a war finally reaches his castle. In the fighting, fully ruthless for the first time, fully faithless to what he swore Silvia, he breaks the contract completely rather than merely straining it. The backlash that follows isn't a jolt this time — it's the Fae King's own entire stake collapsing at once, a blow dealt by his own broken word, and it staggers him badly enough to open the opening a mortal blade never could have found. Someone — unidentified, a casualty of the same war — drives Silvia's spear into his chest.

    Anima's gold in the scale wins the fight inside the wound; Tellus's silver, still trying to heal him out of the same survival instinct that started all of this, loses. Nexus's own blood turns gold as it happens — not because his blood is always gold, but because that is what Anima's Ichor overtaking a dying body looks like. He lies in the ruins of his own throne room long enough to see his descendant, Corvinus, and to bind him into the crown with a formal, active oath before he dies — see the passive/active contract distinction on Nexum for what that means in practice; Corvinus is physically compelled to accept the throne and leave his dying king behind, whether he is ready to or not.

    His death releases the weakened, centuries-corroded Tellus back into the world — not freshly wounded but fully unraveled after all that time — and what sheds from Nexus in that same moment is Nexum itself: the law of binding oaths, no longer contained to the one broken promise that started it, now loosed on the whole world.
