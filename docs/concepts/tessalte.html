    Concepts/Tessalte

    Tessalte

    conceptdevelopingupdated 2026-09-23#concept#cosmology#dimension#agency

    Type: A dimension — real, occupied geometry, not a platonic concept like River of Knowledge Category: Its own fundamental kind of dimension — a third category standing alongside the physical (space) and temporal (time) categories, not a subtype or extra axis of either Structure: 3 axes — Will, Experience, Connection, nested rather than independent (the same nesting relationship length, width, and height have within the physical category) Linked domain: Agency — but Tessalte is the medium Agency's own domain-force writes onto, not the domain itself (see below)

    Not Anima's domain

    Tessalte is a dimension the same way space is — real, occupied geometry, something a being actually occupies a shape within — but it isn't made of the same stuff as physical space, any more than time is. Physical, temporal, and Tessalte are three separate categories of dimension, not three sizes of the same one: length/width/height nest inside the physical category, time stands alone inside the temporal one, and Will/Experience/Connection nest inside the Tessalte category. None of the three is built from either of the others. River of Knowledge is a different kind of thing again: platonic, shapeless, no location or body of its own, only information. Agency's true domain is a force of that second, platonic kind — something that predates and exceeds Anima, the way the River predates and exceeds Aequor — and it is not yet named. Tessalte is only the stage that force writes onto, the way physical space is the stage Tellus's own (also unnamed) domain-force writes onto.

    GapOpen — the two missing domain-objects

    Aequor is the only one of the Three whose platonic domain-object has a name and a page (River of Knowledge). Tellus and Anima only have their mortal-facing mechanics (Nexum, Sacrarium) — which River of Knowledge.md already treats as a different category of thing entirely ("no game mechanic attached, unlike Nectaris, Sacrarium, Nexum"). Candidate directions discussed but not settled: - Tellus's domain-object — a finished, already-written book, mirroring the River's "book still being written" (Cosmology already calls Fate "a closed script rather than a still-growing record" — this would just be naming that book). - Anima's domain-object — a wind/breath image, tying to Anima as dragon of the wind and "agency as breath" (Anima). Until these are named, Tessalte and the physical dimensions are stages without a credited author.

    Structure: Will → Experience → Connection

    The 3 Tessalte axes are not three independent sliders. They nest the way physical dimensions do — a line swept along a second axis makes a plane, a plane swept along a third makes a volume:

    AxisAnalogWhat it is
    Willa linea single scalar pull toward or away from something — the atomic unit; the canvas, a being's core identity, the material every pull is cut from
    Experiencea planeWill swept along a second axis — many pulls held and weighed against each other at once; the paint, what's been lived, weighed against what
    Connectiona volumeExperience swept along a third axis — the resolved, acted-on choice; the one axis that's actually occupied; the tether, always landing toward or away from another being's will

    Moving along Connection is choosing. Moving along Experience is re-weighing what you'd trade for what. Moving along Will is the desire shifting underneath both.

    Will is the texture a being's pulls are made of; Experience is what gets laid onto that canvas over a life; Connection is where the acted-on choice actually lands — never in isolation, always reaching toward or against someone else's island.

    NoteFormerly Want / Cost / Direction

    Earlier drafts named these axes Want, Cost, and Direction, with Will, Experience, and Connection as their "substance." The two layers have since merged — Will, Experience, and Connection are the axes now. Want/Cost/Direction survive only as loose shorthand for what moving along each axis feels like from inside: wanting, weighing, choosing.

    Will formation: contact, not pre-existence

    A will is not a shape that already exists somewhere in Tessalte space, waiting for a body to connect to it — it is generated at the moment of contact. Before a body touches the Tessalte, there is no shape there to find, only the possibility of one. This follows from Tessalte's relationship to time (below): the River of Knowledge is a generative process, not a passive record, and a will is exactly the kind of thing the River generates rather than merely locates.

    Ferus is the clearest case. Made on instinct before Sacrarium existed to carry, he had no will to find — not one that was simply out of reach, but none at all. Agency had to be exercised on him from outside, case by case, precisely because contact had never happened.

    This also settles what a will inherits when contact does happen. A newborn's body has never held a shape before, so the will that forms at contact starts with no prior Experience and no prior Connection. A body that already carries a full history lived without a will present — feral, or otherwise steered from outside — poses the opposite problem: contact generates a will with a body's worth of past attached to it that it did not choose and cannot verify as its own lived experience, only as data about what that body did. Whether that will claims the body's past as its own story, or refuses it, isn't settled by the mechanics — it's an act of Agency the will has to make for itself, the only authorship available to it over a History it didn't write.

    A being's will as a 4D shape

    At any instant, a being's will is a 3D solid in Tessalte space — an extent along Will, Experience, and Connection, not a single point. Dragged forward by time, that solid traces a 4D history the way a physical object traces a worldline through spacetime: the full shape of everything a being wanted, weighed, and chose, laid out across a life.

    Sacrarium as access and magnitude

    Sacrarium's quantity is not a separate fuel source that happens to unlock Tessalte movement — it is the measurement of how much Tessalte volume a being can sustain. Access and strength are the same fact read two ways. This folds Sacrarium's existing mechanics in directly:

    • Depletion (through childbearing, theft, or never inheriting enough) shrinks the volume of will a being has room to occupy.
    • A being with none has zero Tessalte volume — no shape of their own to steer. Ferus, made on instinct alone before Sacrarium existed to carry, reads as exactly this: agency exercised on him from outside, case by case, because there was no dimensionality of his own to move through.
    • In children — sons especially — early contact through Sacrarium reshapes the body itself: the connection becomes structural, so the body no longer needs to keep holding Sacrarium to stay wired into the Tessalte. This is why sons carry only an inert residue rather than an active share — the residue is what's left once the graft has already taken and the fuel has receded, not a weaker version of the gift.
    • A body that has already finished growing before contact cannot take that graft. The connection stays exactly what it was given — a loan, not a remodeling — and reverts once its portion is spent or returns to its source. Ferus receiving Sacrarium as a grown man is this case: not a permanent inheritance but a fixed window, because his body can hold the fuel only for as long as it's actively supplied.
    • Losing that connection is not annihilation — nothing in Sacrarium's mechanics ever destroys outright, only redistributes (portions dissipate and resettle, overflow moves outward, theft displaces). What recedes when contact ends is the same thing a son already carries: a dormant, unnoticeable residue, not a deletion. From outside, that's not death. From inside, it's indistinguishable from it — the only faculty that could register the difference is exactly what's being switched off.

    Relationship to time

    Time is not a dimension standing alongside the physical and Tessalte ones as a seventh peer — it's the process that writes both of them. River of Knowledge is that process, and it runs on A-theory, not B-theory: not a passive coordinate laid out for reality to sit along, but an axis of reality — the active edge where the future is written because it does not yet exist (see River of Knowledge.md's growing-block time).

    Once a page is written, it settles into a fixed record — and from inside that record, both physical and Tessalte content look like they sit along an ordinary passive time-axis, block-universe style. That appearance is downstream of the River's writing, not the fundamental nature of time itself.

    Because the River is the generative process and Tessalte is only ever the thing being generated, the River can reach into Tessalte directly and overwrite it — author a being's will rather than merely record what it chose. This is not a new power grafted on; it's the same authority pattern already running through the myth, where Aequor is consistently the one who acts on his brothers rather than the reverse (Anima's heart, Tellus's death — see Cosmology).

    GapOpen — does the overwrite cost the River anything?

    Every other gift and act in this cosmology is paid for (Cosmology's "a price is always exacted"). Whether a River overwrite costs it something proportionate, or whether History genuinely outranks Agency here and the "equally strong" framing in River of Knowledge needs a caveat, is undecided.
