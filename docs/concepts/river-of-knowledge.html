    Concepts/River of Knowledge

    River of Knowledge

    conceptdevelopingupdated 2026-09-09#concept#aequor#cosmology#ichor#history

    Type: In-universe concept — not physical, not a place or object; made of information itself. No game mechanic attached (unlike Nectaris, Sacrarium, Nexum) Origin: Predates and exceeds the Three — the wellspring Aequor's domain draws from, rather than Aequor himself Domain: History — what has become, and the still-open frontier of what is becoming Also known as: Aequor's Ichor (the in-world name doubling as the working title for this vault's knowledge base)

    Description

    It has no form, no location, and no body — it is not a place one travels to or an object one holds, only information itself, given no shape of its own. Depending on who perceives it, or when, it has been described as a river, a book, a telescope — and none of these are quite right, because it is not any one of them. They are borrowed metaphors, not a true shape:

    > There was a river, no it was a book, no.. a telescope. Well it had no form. The embodiment of knowledge, it was... Where was it? > > Well Where had no meaning here. Time had no meaning here. Yet it was everywhere, and everywhen, but the book wasn't done. The river still flowed, there was yet to discover.

    It is also not complete. Whatever metaphor is active, that metaphor is always mid-motion — a book still being written, a river still flowing downstream, never a finished object. The incompleteness is not a flaw in the description; it is the point. Aequor is not this force, only its channel — the source is older and larger than any of the Three.

    Growing-block time

    The River of Knowledge does not hold a single, already-finished timeline the way a completed record would:

    • The past and present are real and settled — pages already written, water already passed. They do not change.
    • The future does not yet exist. It is not hidden or already decided somewhere; it has simply not been written yet.
    • "The present" only has meaning as the live edge where the book is still being written, where new pages and new water keep arriving. Nothing exists outside the book, because it is never finished enough to be viewed from outside.

    This stands in direct opposition to Tellus's domain of Fate, where the future is already fixed — a closed script rather than a still-growing record. See Cosmology for the fuller three-way split.

    The Three Domains

    Each of the Three holds one domain, equally strong, and each is entangled with the other two rather than sealed off from them:

    DomainGodIchor colorMagic system
    HistoryAequorTeal—
    FateTellusSilverNexum
    AgencyAnimaGoldSacrarium
    • History vs. Fate are direct antitheses: an open, still-being-written future against a closed, predetermined one.
    • Agency is the middle term between them, not a third, unrelated thing. Agency cannot exist under pure Fate — a fully predetermined future leaves nothing left to choose. But agency also can't exist without a self to do the choosing, and a self is built out of a record of the past, which only exists because of History. Agency depends on History, and is opposed by Fate.

    This is the deeper shape behind Aequor training Silvia in the use of her Sacrarium ("let my Ichor flood you" — Lore in order, Ch. 3): History teaching Agency how to use itself. It's also the shape behind Tellus's arc — a being defined by Fate, straining against the possibility of choosing for himself, and unraveling for it.

    Aequor's Ichor (the knowledge base)

    The name doubles as both the in-world force and the out-of-world framing device. The manuscript's prologue is written as if the reader is looking directly into the River/Book itself, before it settles ("flips") to a specific page — the story that follows is framed as pages the Book is turning to, not a disembodied narrator's account.

    The same name extends to the companion knowledge base for this vault: Aequor's Ichor, a "knowledge sea" readers can dive into the way the River/Book itself holds everything, all at once, at whatever depth they choose to go.

    The ~ marker

    A narration convention tied directly to this force. ~ is reserved for two specific moves only — not every scene or chapter break, which stay plain <hr>:

    1. A genuine POV shift to a different character's focus — e.g. the lens focused on ~Nexus.
    2. A time-skip, phrased through water/river imagery rather than stated outright as a mechanic — e.g. the lens flowed down the river 1000 years.

    The glyph is meant to be learned by the reader through repetition across the manuscript, not explained on the page.

    Notes

    • Resolves a stale gap note in Cosmology and Aequor, which had marked Aequor's gift to Primum as merely "inferred" wisdom. Lore in order, Chapter 4, actually states it outright — "Aequor, brother of wisdom, filled Primum with wisdom and resolve" — so the gift itself was never in question, only its underlying domain was. History is that domain: wisdom is what History looks like when handed to a mortal, with real structural consequences behind it (growing-block time, the antithesis to Fate, the dependency Agency has on it).
    • The prologue's refusal to settle on one metaphor (river / book / telescope) is intentional and should stay unresolved on the page.
