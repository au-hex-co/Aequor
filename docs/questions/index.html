Contradictions 2

  • Ferrin vs. Ferus

    This table names the clan "Ferrin" as nearly wiped out in the Aetas of War. The main pitch text names the firstborn "Ferus." See Ferus for the open question of whether these are the same lineage.

    → Timeline of the Aetas
  • Ferus vs. Ferrin

    Timeline of the Aetas refers to a clan called "Ferrin" ("nearly wiped out" during the Aetas of War), while the main pitch text names the firstborn "Ferus." These may be the same lineage (Ferus → clan Ferrin) or a spelling inconsistency in the source document. Confirm with the author and standardize.

    → Ferus

Gaps 5

  • Undefined term: Vesta

    Inheritance of the First Wound references "Vesta" as what Primum's severed arm and leg became. Not defined anywhere else in the source material — see Primum.

    → Nectaris
  • Only one relic is designed so far

    Anima and Aequor have no relic of their own yet, unlike Tellus → Nectaris. A second or third relic tied to the other two gods would round out the pantheon mechanically.

    → Relics
  • Undeveloped

    Aequor has no confirmed gift, relic, or era of worship in the source pitch (unlike Tellus → Nectaris and Terra/Manus eras, or Anima → Aetas of Gods). By elimination Aequor likely gave wisdom (see Cosmology), but this is inferred, not stated. Given the game is naval/ocean-based, Aequor may deserve more direct presence — a second relic, a patron era, or a role in the ending.

    → Aequor (deity)
  • Undeveloped

    Anima has the least detail of the three gods in the source pitch. No relic or gameplay system is tied to him yet, unlike Tellus (→ Nectaris). Worth developing if a second major relic/god-power is planned.

    → Anima
  • Vesta

    The relic ability Inheritance of the First Wound (see Nectaris) says Primum's arm and leg were "torn away to make Vesta." Vesta is not otherwise defined anywhere in the source material — unclear if it's a place, an object, or a being. Needs a definition.

    → Primum

Key Insights 4

  • Design note (from source)

    Sortis, Unbroken and Fate's Reboot draw from the same Deferred Fate queue at different scales — one hit versus one encounter — and should be mutually exclusive as capstone picks, so a player cannot stack two deferral sources into a single future reckoning.

    → Nectaris
  • The rule every relic should follow

    No relic should offer a free escape. Every dodge, reroute, or reboot should queue consequence into a later fight — mechanically enforcing the same rule the mythology states outright: a price is always exacted. This is the load-bearing design constraint for any future relic beyond Nectaris.

    → Relics
  • The current era is a design hook

    The unnamed "Current Era" is explicitly where the player's run(s) take place, and where Cedere's name arc plays out. Whatever the player does may retroactively become the defining event that names this Aetas — a strong narrative frame for run-to-run stakes.

    → Timeline of the Aetas
  • Naming

    The vault itself is named Aequor after this god — the ocean dragon presiding over a pirate/naval game about debts that outlive their gods. Worth leaning into thematically (title screen, splash text, etc.) if it isn't already intentional.

    → Aequor (deity)
