Gaps 6

  • Undeveloped

    The source pitch states the three names and their meaning but not the triggers that move the player between them — whether it's tied to run count, specific choices, or story beats. Needs a design decision before it can drive UI or narrative pacing.

    → The Protagonist
  • Only one relic is designed so far

    Anima and Aequor have no relic of their own yet, unlike Tellus → Nectaris. A second or third relic tied to the other two gods would round out the pantheon mechanically.

    → Relics
  • These 13 founders and their children are new inventions, not drawn from any source document

    Flagging clearly so they don't get mistaken for pre-existing canon later. Fine to treat as placeholder color to be overwritten, or to keep as-is — that's an author call.

    → Lint Report: 2026-09-02

By elimination Aequor likely gave wisdom (see Cosmology), but this is inferred, not stated. Given the game is naval/ocean-based, Aequor may deserve more direct presence — a second relic, a patron era, or a role in the ending.

    → Aequor (deity)

    → Anima

Key Insights 8

  • The arc is not cosmetic

    It mirrors the game's core philosophy — every gift, every mercy, every dodge has a price, and the question is only ever who ends up paying it. See Cosmology and Relics for the same rule expressed mythologically and mechanically.

    → The Protagonist
  • Design note (from source)

    Sortis, Unbroken and Fate's Reboot draw from the same Deferred Fate queue at different scales — one hit versus one encounter — and should be mutually exclusive as capstone picks, so a player cannot stack two deferral sources into a single future reckoning.

    → Nectaris
  • The rule every relic should follow

    No relic should offer a free escape. Every dodge, reroute, or reboot should queue consequence into a later fight — mechanically enforcing the same rule the mythology states outright: a price is always exacted. This is the load-bearing design constraint for any future relic beyond Nectaris.

    → Relics
  • Ferrin vs. Ferus

    This table names the clan "Ferrin" as nearly wiped out in the Aetas of War. The main pitch text names the firstborn "Ferus." See The clans and Ferus — treated as the same lineage (Ferrin = "the line of Ferus"), pending confirmation with the author.

    → Timeline of the Aetas
  • The current era is a design hook

    The unnamed "Current Era" is explicitly where the player's run(s) take place, and where Cedere's name arc plays out. Whatever the player does may retroactively become the defining event that names this Aetas — a strong narrative frame for run-to-run stakes.

    → Timeline of the Aetas
  • Naming

    The vault itself is named Aequor after this god — the ocean dragon presiding over a pirate/naval game about debts that outlive their gods. Worth leaning into thematically (title screen, splash text, etc.) if it isn't already intentional.

    → Aequor (deity)
  • Ferus vs. Ferrin

    Timeline of the Aetas refers to a clan called "Ferrin" ("nearly wiped out" during the Aetas of War), while the main pitch text names the firstborn "Ferus." The clans now treats these as the same lineage — Ferrin as "the line of Ferus" — with one of his children lost defending the clan during the Aetas of War, tying the near-wipeout directly to him. Worth confirming with the author that this reading is the intended one rather than a spelling inconsistency.

    → Ferus
  • The rule beneath the myth

    Every gift in this cosmology is paid for in flesh, blood, or divinity. Relics and Nectaris turn that same rule into a combat mechanic: no relic offers a free escape, only a deferred price.

    → Cosmology
