Gaps 8

  • Undeveloped

    The source pitch states the three names and their meaning but not the triggers that move the player between them — whether it's tied to run count, specific choices, or story beats. Needs a design decision before it can drive UI or narrative pacing.

    → The Protagonist
  • Open thread — a discrimination that isn't in the mechanics

    Nexum isn't sex-locked; a woman can hold, earn, and lose it exactly as a man can. But nothing stops the world's people from acting as if it were — no one extends a woman credit, a contract, or a loan on the strength of her word, regardless of how much Nexum she actually holds. Worth developing deliberately: not a rule of the magic, but a prejudice riding on top of it, mirroring the Sacrarium's own class-discrimination angle (see Sacrarium) from the opposite direction.

    → Nexum
  • Only one relic is designed so far

    Anima and Aequor have no relic of their own yet, unlike Tellus → Nectaris. A second or third relic tied to the other two gods would round out the pantheon mechanically.

    → Relics
  • These 13 founders and their children are new inventions, not drawn from any source document

    Flagging clearly so they don't get mistaken for pre-existing canon later. Fine to treat as placeholder color to be overwritten, or to keep as-is — that's an author call.

    → Lint Report: 2026-09-02
  • Still undeveloped

    Aequor has no confirmed relic or era of worship in the source pitch (unlike Tellus → Nectaris and Terra/Manus eras, or Anima → Aetas of Gods). Given the game is naval/ocean-based, Aequor may deserve more direct presence — a second relic, a patron era, or a role in the ending.

    → Aequor (deity)
  • Undeveloped

    Anima has the least detail of the three gods in the source pitch. No combat relic is tied to him yet, unlike Tellus (→ Nectaris) — though Sacrarium is arguably his mechanical legacy already, just not expressed as a relic. Worth developing if a second major relic/god-power is planned. Whether his sealed, maimed state is ever revisited (does he wake? does anything happen inside that kingdom?) is also open.

    → Anima
  • Undeveloped

    Corvinus exists so far only in the scene of his coronation. His reign, his character beyond that moment, and how "a better king than Nexus" plays out are all open.

    → Corvinus
  • Open threads

    - Nexus's teal eyes mirroring Aequor's, and Silvia's silver mirroring what is now established as Tellus's own Ichor color — both unexplained in-story. Neither sibling is marked by the god they end up bound to; each is marked by the other one. Left as a mystery neither character has solved, not yet given an in-text explanation. - The identity of whoever actually struck the final blow with Silvia's spear — deliberately anonymous, a casualty of the same war, not a named character (yet).

    → Nexus

Key Insights 11

  • The arc is not cosmetic

    It mirrors the game's core philosophy — every gift, every mercy, every dodge has a price, and the question is only ever who ends up paying it. See Cosmology and Relics for the same rule expressed mythologically and mechanically.

    → The Protagonist
  • Design note (from source)

    Sortis, Unbroken and Fate's Reboot draw from the same Deferred Fate queue at different scales — one hit versus one encounter — and should be mutually exclusive as capstone picks, so a player cannot stack two deferral sources into a single future reckoning.

    → Nectaris
  • Ichor is not blood

    Ichor is a god's own magic — the domain itself, not a fluid. Sacrarium is Anima's Ichor (gold). Nexum is Tellus's Ichor (silver). Whatever Aequor carries and channels into Silvia when he trains her (see Lore in order, Ch. 3 — "let my Ichor flood you") is his (teal). Blood is a separate thing — ordinary life-force, not fixed to any of these colors on its own. When Nexus's blood turns gold as he dies (see Nexus), that isn't his blood's natural color; it's Anima's Ichor, carried in the spear that kills him, visibly overtaking everything else in his body as it wins.

    → Nexum
  • The rule every relic should follow

    No relic should offer a free escape. Every dodge, reroute, or reboot should queue consequence into a later fight — mechanically enforcing the same rule the mythology states outright: a price is always exacted. This is the load-bearing design constraint for any future relic beyond Nectaris.

    → Relics
  • Ferrin vs. Ferus

    This table names the clan "Ferrin" as nearly wiped out in the Aetas of War. The main pitch text names the firstborn "Ferus." See The clans and Ferus — treated as the same lineage (Ferrin = "the line of Ferus"), pending confirmation with the author.

    → Timeline of the Aetas
  • The current era is a design hook

    The unnamed "Current Era" is explicitly where the player's run(s) take place, and where Cedere's name arc plays out. Whatever the player does may retroactively become the defining event that names this Aetas — a strong narrative frame for run-to-run stakes.

    → Timeline of the Aetas
  • Naming

    The vault itself is named Aequor after this god — the ocean dragon presiding over a pirate/naval game about debts that outlive their gods. Worth leaning into thematically (title screen, splash text, etc.) if it isn't already intentional.

    → Aequor (deity)
  • Gift resolved (2026-09-09)

    Aequor's gift to Primum is wisdom — stated outright in Lore in order, Ch. 4 ("Aequor, brother of wisdom, filled Primum with wisdom and resolve"), not merely inferred as previously noted here. The domain underneath that gift is History: see River of Knowledge for the formless source force, growing-block time, and how History/Fate/Agency form an equally-weighted triangle across the Three.

    → Aequor (deity)
  • Ferus vs. Ferrin

    Timeline of the Aetas refers to a clan called "Ferrin" ("nearly wiped out" during the Aetas of War), while the main pitch text names the firstborn "Ferus." The clans now treats these as the same lineage — Ferrin as "the line of Ferus" — with one of his children lost defending the clan during the Aetas of War, tying the near-wipeout directly to him. Worth confirming with the author that this reading is the intended one rather than a spelling inconsistency.

    → Ferus
  • Ichor vs. blood

    Ichor is a god's own magic — the domain itself, colored per god — not a bodily fluid. Anima's is gold (Sacrarium); Tellus's is silver (Nexum); Aequor's is teal. Blood is ordinary life-force, a separate thing entirely, not fixed to any of these colors — when a scene shows divine blood running one of these colors, it means that god's Ichor is actively present or winning, not that blood is inherently that color.

    → Cosmology
  • The rule beneath the myth

    Every gift in this cosmology is paid for in flesh, blood, or divinity. Relics and Nectaris turn that same rule into a combat mechanic: no relic offers a free escape, only a deferred price.

    → Cosmology

Other notes 1

  • Not a symmetrical origin, on purpose

    Anima's sacrifice was chosen, consenting, dignified. Tellus's fall is temper, panic, and failure. That asymmetry is deliberate, not an oversight — if every god's ending were a noble self-sacrifice, the pattern would read as a formula the story reaches for on demand rather than two different deaths earning two different weights.

    → Nexum
